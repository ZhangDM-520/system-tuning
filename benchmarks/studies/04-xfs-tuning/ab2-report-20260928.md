## Phase A — `allocsize=1m` (treatment) vs dynamic default (control), workload `wbig`

### Arm definitions & readback proofs

| Arm | Definition (intent) | Readback proof in the rows |
|---|---|---|
| Control | dynamic default XFS allocation size (root mount, no `allocsize`) | `cfg.mount_options = rw,lazytime,relatime,inode64,logbufs=8,logbsize=256k,noquota`, no `mount.allocsize` key — consistent with dynamic default |
| Treatment | `mount -o remount,allocsize=1m /` | **MISSING.** All 6 `ab2-alloc-1m-*` rows (incl. warmup) record the *identical* `mount_options` as control — no `allocsize=` option, no `cfg.mount.allocsize` key |

`tools/fslat.py` `capture_settings()` snapshots `/proc/mounts` at run start and explicitly probes for
`allocsize=` (fslat.py:254). The absence in every treatment row therefore means one of:

1. the remount was never applied / did not stick at run time (arm-identity failure, METHODOLOGY §3
   "measuring the wrong thing"), or
2. `allocsize` is remount-inert or invisible in `/proc/mounts` on kernel 7.3-rc4-cachyos-lto
   (the `filestreams` option had exactly this behaviour — rc=0, never visible, inert).

An 8 GiB loopback XFS image (`~/.cache/bench-scratch/allocloop/img`, mtime 14:17, *after* Phase A
results at 14:13) suggests a remount-visibility test was being prepared, but **no test result is on
disk**. Either way the METHODOLOGY §3 rule ("read a setting back from where the consumer reads it")
is not satisfied: the treatment arm is not identified.

Both arms held `fs.xfs.xfssyncd_centisecs=10000` / `fs.xfs.speculative_prealloc_lifetime=300`
(constant across Phase A — correct isolation of the allocsize variable).

### True n & design

- n = **5/arm measured** (`ab2-alloc-ctrl-wbig`, `ab2-alloc-1m-wbig`), plus 1 warmup/arm
  (`*-warmup`, pair0) excluded per the rep-0/warmup rule.
- Interleaved + rotated: pair1 ctrl-pos1/1m-pos2, pair2 1m-pos1/ctrl-pos2, alternating to pair5.
  Each `fslat.py run` invocation = 1 repetition (rows all carry `rep: 1`, `note: pairN-posM`).
- Cold cache discipline per campaign design (drop_caches before each run), `wl.sh wbig` =
  `dd bs=1M count=4096` + `sync` — fixed 4 GiB of work per run, so J/work is J per GiB.

### Gates & verdict

- **Gate 1 (effect > control's own spread%): FAIL on every metric.** The largest effects (PSI
  −14.8/−15.4%) sit deep inside their control spreads (37.7/36.3%).
- **Gate 2 (MW p<0.05): passes only write ios (p=0.028)** — but that "effect" is 10 I/Os on ~32 860
  (−0.03%), 3× *inside* the control's own 0.1% spread. Per METHODOLOGY §4 an effect inside the
  control spread is not a result regardless of p; a rank test on nearly-identical counts can separate
  at n=5. Spurious-multiple-comparison class (1 of 17 metrics tested).
- Nothing significantly worse (no Gate-1-passing regressions).
- **Verdict: NOT ADOPTED — gates fail at n=5/arm.** Ceiling note: with n=5 the verdict ceiling is
  *not* the INCONCLUSIVE-lean cap; the gates themselves decide, and they say "no separable effect".
  Separately, because the treatment arm's readback is missing, this null result **cannot refute**
  `allocsize=1m` either — the correct reading is "campaign did not measure an identified
  allocsize=1m arm". A re-run with per-row `mount.allocsize` readback (or a loopback visibility proof
  first) is required before any allocsize verdict, positive or negative.

### True n, gates, verdict

- **True n = 2/arm** (ctrl w1+w4, treat w2+w3). w0 excluded as warmup (planned `warmup-discarded`),
  w5 excluded (see history & unknowns; row annotated `ab2-idle-ctrl-EXCLUDED` with
  `excluded_reason`: "contaminated: owner session active (backlight 302920 vs baseline 120000),
  log_force=798/flush=877 vs ~100 baseline, package power 5-24W vs 2-3W").
- **Gates: screening status only.** Hard ceiling rule: n<5/arm → verdict is capped at
  INCONCLUSIVE-lean → **NO adoption, regardless of p-values.** At n=2 vs 2 a Mann-Whitney U test's
  minimum achievable two-sided p is 0.333 — Gate 2 is structurally unpassable at this n.
- Screening medians (usable set): full-window J/min ctrl 129.25 vs treat 127.70 (−1.2%);
  flush/min 8.40 vs 8.40 (0.0%); log FORCE/min 8.16 vs 8.16 (0.0%); log blocks/min 472.5 vs 510.4
  (+8.0%, wrong direction); trim events 0 vs 0; free-dip 188 vs 1 182 KB (treat deeper, n=2, no
  mechanism — no prealloc overhang existed to hold space).
- **Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE-lean (ceiling: n=2/arm) → NO adoption.**

### Mechanism attribution — flush cadence vs trim cadence

- **Flush cadence (`xfssyncd_centisecs` 100 s → 300 s): NOT demonstrated.** If xfssyncd wakeups
  dominated log forcing, control (100 s cadence) should force the log ~3× more often than
  treatment (300 s cadence) during idle. Measured idle-phase (xstats, 60 s→720 s span) log FORCE:
  ctrl 7.97 & 7.60/min (med 7.79) vs treat 8.69 & 7.33/min (med 8.01) — ratio ≈ 1.0, treat not
  lower. Idle flush/min: ctrl 8.24/7.87 vs treat 8.97/7.51. The idle spans carry ~800–1 100 write
  ios and ~83–99 flush ios per 11 min even with the panel dimmed — background/owner traffic swamps
  any periodic-wakeup signal. The knob did not move flush cadence above the noise floor here.
- **Trim cadence (`speculative_prealloc_lifetime` 300 s → 1800 s): NOT EXERCISED.** Zero trim events
  in every window (both arms) and `big_blocks` constant at exactly 8 388 608 (= the 4 GiB `dd` data
  size) in **every sample of every window** at 5 s resolution — i.e. no post-EOF speculative prealloc
  overhang was ever allocated for this workload (`dd` buffered write + `sync` writes exactly to EOF),
  so control had nothing to trim at ~300 s and treatment nothing to defer. Time-to-first-trim is
  undefined in both arms. The prelife contrast is untested in practice with `wbig`.
- Consequently the ~1% within-pair J/min deltas cannot be attributed to either policy mechanism.

## Design-change history

The Phase B design began with planned 12–15 min idle windows and was shortened mid-campaign to
**750 s** (all measured windows ran 750.0 s). The original interleaved+rotated 10-window driver
(`ab2-phaseB-driver.sh`: w0 warmup + 5 pairs of ctrl/treat, arms rotated per position) was
**killed mid-window-5**; single-window resume drivers were written (`ab2-phaseB-driver2.sh`, then
`ab2-phaseB-driver3.sh` which adds a pre-window quiet gate: backlight idle-dimmed 120000 + package
power < 4.5 W before starting). Window 5 (control, pair3-pos1) is **excluded**: its data files are
shape-complete on disk (150 samples / 12 xstats over ~750 s) but the window is flagged partial/
contaminated — its summary row (annotated by the interrupted agent) records owner-session activity
(bright backlight 302920 vs 120000 baseline), log force/flush ≈ 8× baseline, and 685.7 J/min
full-window vs 98.6–145.5 for clean windows. The host then **lost power (battery died)** while this
report work was in flight; per the power-loss rule every JSONL was scanned for a trailing truncated
line — **none found; all files parse completely** (if a partial line had existed it would have been
dropped and only valid rows counted). Windows 6–14 of the driver schedule never ran, so Phase B
stopped at **n=2/arm usable**.

## Screen-blanking handling

- **Sampling method:** backlight `brightness` + `actual_brightness` recorded every 5 s per window
  (plus the sampler's own per-sample records). Protocol metric "screen-off-only J/min" = largest
  contiguous run (gap ≤ 10 s) of samples with `bl == 0 AND bl_act == 0`, after trimming the first
  180 s; computed from RAPL `energy_uj` deltas over that run. Full-window J/min = whole-window RAPL
  delta ÷ wall × 60.
- **Finding: zero samples with `bl == 0` in any window (w0–w5).** The panel never fully blanked
  inside a 750 s window. Observed states: idle-dimmed `(120000, 120000)`, transitions
  `(120000, 49506)` / `(120000, 149373)`, owner-active bright `(302920, 249788)`.
- **Screen-on coverage per arm** (strict protocol definition, `bl>0 or bl_act>0`): **100% in every
  window both arms** (sampler's `screen_on_frac` = 1.00 for all rows). Bright-state coverage
  (`bl>120000`, the ~13 W regime): ctrl w1 18.0% (27/150), w4 7.3% (11/150); treat w2 24.7%
  (37/150), w3 2.0% (3/149); excluded w5 92.7% (139/150). Warmup w0 0%.
- **Are J comparisons valid? NO — not for gating.** (1) The primary screen-off-only J/min is
  unavailable everywhere (no screen-off samples). (2) Full-window J/min is confounded by unmatched
  bright coverage and the measured ~13 W panel regime shift — full-window values track the screen
  regime almost exactly (w1/w2 at 18–25% bright read 145.5/143.8 J/min; w3/w4 at 2–7% bright read
  111.6/113.0 J/min), which is larger than any arm delta. (3) For transparency, a non-protocol
  sensitivity metric (largest contiguous idle-dimmed `(120000,120000)` run after the 180 s trim)
  gives J/min_dim: ctrl 95.79 / 103.25 (med 99.52) vs treat 97.27 / 109.18 (med 103.23) — treat
  +1.5% and +5.7% within the two pairs (wrong direction for an energy win), with unequal segment
  lengths (286–568 s). Energy is reportable only as a noise-floor/screening statement.

## Blockers / unknowns

1. **Phase A arm identity (blocker for any allocsize verdict):** no `allocsize=1m` readback in any
   treatment row. Unknown whether the remount was never applied or is inert/hidden on 7.3-rc4
   (filestreams precedent). The `allocloop/` 8 GiB loopback image (prepared after Phase A) has no
   recorded test outcome. Phase A is at best screening evidence; it neither supports nor refutes
   `allocsize=1m`.
2. **Phase B n=2/arm:** verdict ceiling INCONCLUSIVE-lean (hard rule); windows 6–14 never ran.
3. **Screen-off-only energy unavailable:** panel never blanked within any 750 s window; primary
   energy metric cannot be computed; J comparisons confounded by screen regime.
4. **Trim mechanism unexercised:** `wbig` (`dd` + `sync`) produced zero post-EOF prealloc overhang at
   5 s resolution — `speculative_prealloc_lifetime` had nothing to act on. Whether XFS speculative
   prealloc overhang occurs for other write patterns (append-heavy buffered writes, no trailing
   `sync`) is untested.
5. **Idle-window background traffic:** ~800–1 100 write ios / 11 min even dimmed (owner session);
   the pre-window quiet gate (driver3) existed only after w5 was first attempted; w1–w4 ran without
   it. This noise floor (~8 log FORCE/min) is what the flush-cadence effect would have to beat.
6. **Window 5 provenance:** campaign history labels it partial/interrupted; on-disk artifacts are
   shape-complete but contaminated (owner activity, 8× counters, 685.7 J/min). Excluded on both
   readings; no usable signal either way.
